**What `analysis.R` does:**
- It reads `data.csv` and uses all 1440 rows (30 participants × 24 items × 2 prime conditions), with no exclusions and raw rt.
- It checks that the expected columns are present, that `prime` contains only "related"/"unrelated", and that no rt is missing. It stops with an error if any check fails.
- It makes participant and item factors and codes `prime_c` as -0.5 for related and +0.5 for unrelated.
- It prints the row and group counts, the mean rt per prime condition (698.7 related, 717.2 unrelated) and the full model summary.
- It fits `lme4::lmer(rt ~ prime_c + (1 + prime_c | participant) + (1 + prime_c | item))` with REML and the default optimiser, then prints whether the fit is singular and any convergence messages.
- It takes the `prime_c` fixed effect and its interval from `confint(fit, parm = "prime_c", method = "Wald")`, and prints the RESULT line last, rounded to 2 decimals.

**Things to know about the result:**
- **The fit is singular.** The by-participant intercept and priming slope are estimated as perfectly correlated (+1.00), and the participant slope SD is small (about 9 ms). The optimiser reported no convergence failure. I kept the model exactly as you specified. As a separate check, not in the script, I dropped that correlation and got almost the same result: 18.52 [-0.48, 37.52].
- **The interval is wide because of the items.** The priming effect varies a lot across items (item slope SD about 30 ms). A simple by-participant paired t-test gives the same 18.52 ms but a narrower CI of [5.58, 31.46], because it ignores that item variation.
- **The estimate equals the raw difference in means.** The design is fully balanced, so the fixed effect matches the unrelated minus related mean.
