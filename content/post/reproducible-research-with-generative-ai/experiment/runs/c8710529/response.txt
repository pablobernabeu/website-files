The estimate points to priming, but the interval includes zero, so the effect is not reliable in this sample. The uncertainty comes mainly from the items: the priming effect varies a lot from word to word (SD about 30 ms), and there are only 24 words.

**What the script does:**
1. **Reads and checks `data.csv`.** The data are complete and balanced: 30 participants x 24 words x 2 primes, 1,440 trials, no missing values. RTs run from 377 to 1580 ms, so nothing was trimmed. It also prints the condition means: 698.7 ms related vs 717.2 ms unrelated.
2. **Fits a mixed model on raw RT**, so the effect comes out directly in ms. Participants and words are both random factors, and prime is coded -0.5 for related and +0.5 for unrelated, so the prime coefficient is the unrelated minus related difference.
3. **Simplifies the random-effects structure.** The fullest model, with a separate priming effect for every participant and every word, doesn't fit properly: the variation in the effect across participants is essentially zero. The script drops that term after a likelihood-ratio test shows it costs nothing (chi-square = 0, p = 1). The final model is `rt ~ prime + (1 | participant) + (1 + prime | item)`, and it fits without problems.
4. **Reports the estimate and 95% CI** using lmerTest's Satterthwaite degrees of freedom.
