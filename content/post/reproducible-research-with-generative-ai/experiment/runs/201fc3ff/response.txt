Responses after unrelated primes were **18.52 ms** slower than after related primes, with a 95% Wald CI of **[-0.74, 37.78] ms**. The interval includes zero, so the effect is not significant at the .05 level (t = 1.88, SE = 9.83).


**What the script does:**
- Reads `data.csv` and uses all 1440 rows (30 participants × 24 items × 2 primes), with no exclusions and rt left untransformed. The data were complete and balanced.
- Codes `prime_c` as -0.5 for related and +0.5 for unrelated, so its coefficient is the unrelated minus related difference.
- Fits `lmer(rt ~ prime_c + (1 + prime_c | participant) + (1 + prime_c | item))` with REML and the default optimiser.
- Prints the cell means, the model summary, any convergence messages and whether the fit is singular.
- Takes the estimate from `fixef` and the interval from `confint(fit, parm = "prime_c", method = "Wald")`, printing the RESULT line last.

**Caveats:**
- **Singular fit:** lme4 reports a boundary (singular) fit. The participant random slope is small (SD about 8.9 ms) and its correlation with the intercept is estimated at +1.00, so the participant random-effects structure is over-specified for these data. The optimiser still converged (code 0), and I kept the model exactly as specified.
- **Estimate matches the raw means:** because the design is fully balanced, the fixed effect equals the raw difference in means (717.25 − 698.73 ms). The random effects widen the confidence interval but don't change the point estimate.
- **Skewed residuals:** rt was left untransformed as instructed, and the residuals are right-skewed (largest scaled residual about 5.8). This is typical for response times.
