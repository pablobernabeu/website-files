**What analysis.R does:**
1. Reads `data.csv` and checks that the columns are there, `rt` has no missing values, and `prime` has only the levels related and unrelated. The data passed: 1440 rows, 30 participants, 24 items, each participant-item pair seen once per prime, no missing values.
2. Adds `prime_c`, coded -0.5 for related and +0.5 for unrelated. It keeps every row and leaves `rt` untransformed.
3. Prints the mean RT for each condition (related 698.73 ms, unrelated 717.25 ms).
4. Fits `lmer(rt ~ prime_c + (1 + prime_c | participant) + (1 + prime_c | item))` with REML and the default optimiser, prints the model summary, and reports any convergence or singular-fit messages.
5. Takes the fixed effect of `prime_c` and its 95% interval from `confint(fit, parm = "prime_c", method = "Wald")`, then prints the RESULT line.

**The model fit is singular.** lme4 warns about a boundary fit because the by-participant intercept and prime slope are estimated as perfectly correlated (r = 1.00). The prime slope varies very little across participants (SD ≈ 8.9 ms). I kept the model exactly as specified. In a separate check outside the script, the estimate stayed at 18.52 ms and the interval barely moved when I:
- switched to the bobyqa optimiser: CI -0.74 to 37.78
- removed the correlation for participants: CI -0.48 to 37.52
- dropped the participant prime slope: CI -0.48 to 37.52

Because the design is balanced, the estimate equals the raw difference between the condition means.
