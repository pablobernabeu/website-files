The priming effect is about **18.5 ms**: responses were that much slower after unrelated primes than after related ones. The 95% confidence interval is roughly **6 to 31 ms**.

**How I got it.** Each participant saw every target once in each condition, so for every participant and item I took the unrelated RT minus the related RT. That gives 720 paired differences, all done by hand. They add up to 13,334 ms, so the mean difference is 13,334 / 720 = 18.52 ms.

**Confidence interval.** I based it on participant-level means. I averaged each participant's 24 differences, then took the mean and spread of those 30 values.
- The participant priming effects range from about −57 ms (P12) to +77 ms (P21). Most are positive.
- Their standard deviation is about 32.9 ms, so the standard error is 32.9 / √30 ≈ 6.0 ms.
- With t(29) = 2.045, the margin is ±12.3 ms, which gives a 95% CI of [6.2, 30.8].

**Caveats.**
- All the arithmetic was done by hand, so small slips are possible.
- This interval only accounts for variation between participants. Item variability, raw-RT skew and a few very slow responses (over 1,400 ms) would also matter. A crossed mixed-effects model (random intercepts and priming slopes for participants and items), possibly on log RT, would probably give a similar estimate with a somewhat wider interval.
- I did not adjust for word frequency. Because every item appears in both conditions, frequency is balanced across them and does not bias the estimate.
