I wrote and ran `<run folder>/analysis.R`. Responses after unrelated primes were about 21.8 ms slower than after related primes, 95% Wald CI [0.6, 43.0]. The fit was singular, so treat the interval with some caution.

**What the script does:**
1. Reads `data.csv` and uses all 1,354 rows (30 participants, 24 items), with no exclusions and no transformation of rt. It checks the columns and that there are no missing rt values.
2. Prints the raw mean rt for each condition: 698.8 ms after related primes and 720.2 ms after unrelated primes.
3. Codes `prime_c` as -0.5 for related and +0.5 for unrelated.
4. Fits `lmer(rt ~ prime_c + (1 + prime_c | participant) + (1 + prime_c | item))` with REML and the default optimiser, then prints the model summary and any convergence messages.
5. Takes the fixed effect of `prime_c` and its interval from `confint(fit, parm = "prime_c", method = "Wald")`.

**Result:** the estimate is 21.79 ms (SE 10.82, t = 2.01). The last line the script prints is:
`RESULT estimate=21.7914 lower=0.5848 upper=42.9979`

**Singular fit:** the optimiser converged, but the fit is singular ("boundary (singular) fit"). The by-participant random slope for prime has a small variance (SD 6.7 ms) and a correlation of 1.00 with the participant intercept, so the participant random-effects structure is at a boundary. As you specified, the script keeps the model as given and only reports this; it does not simplify the random effects.
