**Priming effect (unrelated minus related): about 18.5 ms, 95% Wald CI roughly −0.5 to 37.5 ms.**

I had only the file-reading tool, so this is a hand calculation, not an lme4 fit.

**Point estimate.** The design is fully balanced: every one of the 30 participants saw all 24 items once in each prime condition. In that case the fixed effect of prime_c from this model equals the mean of the 720 within-pair differences (unrelated rt minus related rt). Those differences sum to 13,334 ms, so the estimate is 13,334 / 720 = 18.52 ms. I checked this sum two ways, by adding up the participant totals and the item totals, and both give 13,334. I am confident in this figure.

**Interval.** This part is approximate. I ran a crossed participant × item ANOVA on the differences:
- **Participant mean square:** about 28,800. This is smaller than the residual, so the participant random-slope variance should come out at or near zero.
- **Item mean square:** about 67,600. This is clearly larger than the residual, giving an item slope variance of about 900. Item W11 alone contributes a total difference of −2,538 ms.
- **Residual mean square:** about 41,000. This comes from a sum of squares I calculated with rounded values.

With the participant slope variance at zero, the standard error is about √(67,640 / 720) ≈ 9.7 ms. That gives 18.5 ± 1.96 × 9.7, or about −0.5 to 37.5 ms. If lmer instead estimates a small positive participant slope variance, for example through the intercept–slope correlation, the interval would be slightly wider.

In short, unrelated primes slow responses by about 18.5 ms on average. The interval only just includes zero, so this estimate does not settle whether there is a priming effect.
